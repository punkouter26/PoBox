PoBox · Unity 6000.6 · MuJoCo 3.5.0 · as of 4 October 2026

Architecture Map

PoBox is a boxing broadcast. Two boxers, each a trained neural network driving a physics body, fight in a ring while the game films, scores and commentates. This page maps the parts, follows the main journeys step by step, and shows what data is kept where.

ComponentsJourneysDataIn plain English

Components

Three groups. The trainer (Python, on the PC) teaches each boxer. The bridge (a Unity editor tool) copies a trained boxer into the project and proves it behaves the same. The game (Unity) runs the fight. One physics model file is used on both sides, which is why a boxer that stands in training stands in the game.

Trainer

Thousands of copies of the ring run at once on the GPU. The learner tries actions, the practice world pays or charges for what happened, and the brain's numbers are nudged towards what paid. Three practice worlds teach three skills.

Bridge

MjRetrofit builds each boxer's prefab from the trainer's model and copies in the brains. The gate tests then replay a recording made in plain MuJoCo and fail if Unity's boxer answers differently.

Game

MjRing owns the fight's physics loop. Fighter keeps score of damage and decides nothing. Everything the audience sees and hears listens to SimBus and never reaches into the physics.

Files that travel with a boxer

In Assets/Boxers/NAME: three prefabs (plain, red kit, blue kit), config.json (joints, strength, rules), three brains (policy.onnx, getup.onnx, match.onnx), fingerprint.json (what the trainer's model must compile to) and recordings from plain MuJoCo that the gate tests replay.

In plain English

A fight, step by step

  1. Pick two boxers

    The menu shows six cards for each corner. The same boxer cannot take both. FIGHT remembers the pick and loads the arena.

  2. The arena wakes two of twelve

    Every boxer waits in the scene twice, once in red kit and once in blue, switched off. MatchSetup switches on the two that were picked before the physics is built, so only those two are simulated.

  3. Each boxer looks

    Fifty times a second a boxer reads 103 numbers: its own balance and joints, where the opponent's head, body and gloves are, and where it stands in the ring.

  4. Its brain answers

    The brain file turns those numbers into 21 joint targets: where each joint should move to next. Nothing is animated or scripted.

  5. Muscles pull, physics decides

    Springy joint drives pull towards the targets with human strength. MuJoCo steps the bodies four times before the next decision. If a boxer is dazed or tired its drives weaken.

  6. The ring watches the gloves

    MjRing checks every glove against the other boxer's head and body. A glove arriving fast enough is a hit, and its speed is its strength.

  7. A hit becomes a story

    Fighter turns the hit into damage and daze and announces it on SimBus. Judges score it, the HUD shows it, the director may cut to the impact camera, the crowd reacts.

  8. Down, and up again

    Enough daze and the legs go. The referee counts. The boxer's get-up brain takes over, stands it up, and after a second and a half the boxing brain has the body back.

  9. A result

    Three rounds of 45 seconds. A count of ten is a knockout. Otherwise three judges (power, volume, craft) give a decision, and the ladder is updated.

How a boxer is made

  1. A mesh becomes a body

    A rigged character file is measured. Simple shapes are fitted inside it and given the mass a person of that size would have.

  2. It learns to stand, walk and turn

    In thousands of practice rings at once, while being shoved and having cubes thrown at it.

  3. It learns to get up

    Dropped with slack joints, with a helping hand at first that fades as it improves.

  4. It learns to box

    Against the other boxers, who are frozen as they are. Landing punches pays; being hit, falling and wasting effort cost.

  5. It sits an exam

    Nine lines, in plain MuJoCo with no luck added. A run that makes any line worse is rejected and the earlier brain stays.

  6. It is checked in Unity

    The gate replays a recording. Unity's boxer must give the same answers, walk the same run and fall as rarely as the recording did. All six pass, 18 checks of 18.
