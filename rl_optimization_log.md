## Phase 1 — audit, instrumentation, baseline

### 1.1 What was audited

| Artefact | Lines | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `Tools/MuJoCo/nick_unity.xml` | 130 | the model Unity's `MjScene` generates; 16 bodies, 30 hinges + free root, 30 position actuators, 75.00 kg |
| `Tools/MuJoCo/nick_env.py` | 760 | mujoco_warp batch env, rsl_rl `VecEnv`; 127 obs / 30 act |
| `Tools/MuJoCo/train_nick.py` | 194 | rsl_rl PPO, 512-256-128 ELU actor and critic |
| `Tools/MuJoCo/eval_nick.py` | 174 | fresh-start survival tables, domain randomisation off |

**MJCF.** Total mass 75.00 kg over 16 bodies (torso 24.3, pelvis 10.3, thighs
8.46 each, head 5.15) — realistic for the size, which AGENTS.md requires.
Every hinge is `limited="true"` with anatomical ranges (knee 0–130°, hip pitch
−110–20°, ankle pitch −40–25°, elbow 0–140°), `damping="2"`, `armature="0.2"`.
Solver Newton / 20 iterations / `implicitfast` / pyramidal cone, timestep
0.005 in the file and overridden per run by `NICK_TIMESTEP`.

Two things the audit checklist asks about are already handled, and one is not:

- **Parent/child contact exclusion** — already on. `<option><flag
  filterparent="enable">` is MuJoCo's own version of `<contact><exclude>` for
  every adjacent pair, so hand-written excludes would be redundant.
- **Non-adjacent self-collision is NOT excluded.** Every geom is
  `contype=1 conaffinity=1`. The upper-arm capsules sit 4 mm clear of the
  torso capsule (x = ±0.156, r = 0.042, torso r = 0.11), so arm-vs-torso
  contacts fire constantly during a swing. Worth excluding — but the MJCF is
  **generated by Unity**, so an exclude added here and not in
  `RigTool_NickMuJoCo.ExportNickMjcf` would train a creature the game does not
  run. **Then measured instead of argued** (`contact_probe.py`, CPU MuJoCo,
  the shipping `nick_balance_002.onnx` driving one world for 20 s standing and
  20 s walking): **one body-body contact in 2,000 steps**, a single
  ShinR/ThighL touch during the walk. There is nothing to exclude. The 4 mm
  clearance never closes because the policy never swings the arm inward that
  far. Closed, not deferred.
- **Foot contact is a line, not a sole.** The same probe counts a mean of 1.11
  floor contacts while standing on two feet: the feet are *capsules*, so each
  touches the floor along an edge, and Nick balances on what is close to a
  point of support. That is the single biggest physical reason balance here is
  hard, and it is not fixable from the trainer -- the geom comes from Unity's
  exporter. Recorded for whoever next edits the rig.
- **Solver stability at 0.02 s** is a solved problem in this repo and the
  solution is in the body, not the trainer: `armature=0.2` on all 30 hinges
  drops `omega*dt` from 2.83 to 0.89 at kp=400. Confirmed present in the
  committed MJCF.

**Observation vector (127).** 13 root terms (height, pelvis-local linear and
angular velocity, up, forward) + 98 joint terms (14 local quaternions and
angular velocities) + 8 foot-contact + 2 foot heights + 6 locomotion command.
It is `CreatureSentisController.GatherObservations` term for term, and
`parity_check.py` measured max element-wise diff 6e-8 on 2026-09-07. **Not
pruned or renormalised**: the 8 foot-contact terms carry only 2 bits (each
flag is repeated four times) and could be 2 terms, but the width is a contract
shared with a C# builder and every exported `.onnx`. Not worth 6 free terms.

**Reward.** Weighted geometric mean of upright, height, speed-match, facing,
single-support, foot clearance, alternation, planted-at-zero-command, minus an
action-rate term, times `control_dt`; −2 on falling. Gait factors fade in with
commanded speed, `planted` fades out.

### 1.2 What was missing, and was added

The reward has no actuator-effort term and the logging had no physical
stability KPIs at all. Added to `nick_env.py` (`extras["log"]`, GPU-side
reductions, no host sync in the control step):

| Metric | What it answers |
|---|---|
| `Metrics/tilt_deg` | torso tilt in degrees — `upright` in a readable unit |
| `Metrics/actuator_force_abs`, `_max` | effort actually **delivered** by the servos, N·m |
| `Metrics/action_cost`, `Metrics/action_rate` | commanded magnitude and chatter |
| `Metrics/joint_accel_abs` | hinge `qacc` — the jerk tell |
| `Metrics/track_err_frac`, `Metrics/track_within_10pct` | velocity tracking as an error and as the ±10 % band the exit criterion names |
| `Metrics/overlift_factor` | how far the swing foot goes past the clearance target |

Two optional reward terms, both **default 0 so the baseline reward is
bit-identical** (`factor(x, 0) == 1`):

- `w_overlift` / `overlift_target` / `overlift_kernel` — the over-lift kernel
  `Tools/MuJoCo/README.md` says is missing. The clearance factor saturates at
  0.10 m and nothing above it costs anything, which is why Nick walks with a
  0.3 m march.
- `w_ctrl_cost` — subtractive on `mean(actions²)`; dimensionless, so it does
  not track the actuator gains.

Also added, because experiments should not need code edits:
`train_nick.py --env KEY=VALUE` and `--train KEY=VALUE` override any
`NickEnvCfg` field or PPO hyperparameter, and `config.json` now records the
**physics block** (timestep, control_dt, integrator, iterations, armature,
total mass). It did not before — `nick04/07/08/config.json` record
`decimation: 1` and leave the reader to infer the 0.02 s step.

`eval_nick.py` gained a **RING table** and the KPI line. The ring is not a
shove rig: `Systems_HazardDirector` also runs 55 N wind gusts held 2 s and a
2.5° gravity lean on a 13 s cycle. The evaluator only shoved, which is how a
brain measured at 95 % full-cap could post a 4.4 s median in the actual ring.

#### Finding 1 — "nick_balance_002 CANNOT WALK" is a measurement artefact

`Assets/Agents/Nick_Balance002/SOURCE.txt` and `CLAUDE.md` both record
"CANNOT WALK: 0 % full-cap, 2.22 s median", and CLAUDE.md builds an
architectural claim on it ("Nick has two brains for the same reason").
Measured at its own 0.02 s step, nick08 walks: **83 % full-cap, 0.893 m/s,
alternation 1.000, 16.8 m in 20 s**. The 0 % figure is what you get by running
`eval_nick.py` without `NICK_TIMESTEP=0.02 NICK_DECIMATION=1`, i.e. by
evaluating a 0.02 s brain at 0.005 s — the same out-of-distribution failure
CLAUDE.md documents in the other direction. Note `eval_nick.py` printed no
timestep line before today, so nothing in the output said which step was used.
It does now.

**Reproduced deliberately.** nick08 model_1499 run at 0.005 × decimation 4:

| Table | at its own 0.02 s | forced to 0.005 s |
|---|---|---|
| BALANCE full-cap | 91 % | 100 % |
| WALK full-cap | **82 %** | **0 %** (median 3.16 s) |

The recorded pair — balance fine, walk 0 % at a ~2–3 s median — is exactly the
0.005 s column. Both documented numbers were taken at the wrong step; the
balance one happened to survive the mistake and the walk one did not.

The corollary is worth stating: **one 0.02 s brain already covers both
mini-games at the ring's locked `fixedDeltaTime`**, which the "two brains, and
that is the architecture" note says is impossible.

#### Finding 2 — the ring hazards are the real gap, and no brain has trained on them

RING is where both brains fall over: 52 % (nick08) and 12 % (nick06) against
89 % and 33 % for the same shoves without gusts and lean. `nick08/config.json`
has **no hazard keys at all** — the wind and lean model was added to
`nick_env.py` after nick08 trained. So the headline number is a policy meeting
a disturbance class it has never seen.

That makes RING full-cap the KPI to optimise, and it is worth more than the
others: it is the number the shipping mini-game actually produces.

#### Finding 3 — the RING table was harsher than the ring

Written first as all three hazards at once. `Systems_HazardDirector` "rolls
one random hazard per round" (its own comment, and `_activeHazard` is a single
int), and `nick_env` samples one mode per episode to match. The evaluator now
does the same: a third of the worlds get shoves only, a third gusts, a third
the lean, and the per-hazard split is printed as well as the mixture. The
Phase 1 RING numbers above are from the all-at-once version and are re-measured
in 2.1.

### 1.4 Throughput baseline

| Configuration | Throughput |
|---|---|
| `nick_env.py` smoke, 4096 worlds, zero actions, no learning | **247,555 env-steps/s** (control steps; ×4 physics substeps) |
| PPO, 4096 worlds, dt 0.02 × dec 1, 24 steps/env | **187,153 steps/s** at the start of a run, **139,474 steps/s** by iteration 700 |
| `nick_env.py` smoke, **8192 worlds**, zero actions | **604,766 env-steps/s** — 2.44× the 4096 figure for 2× the worlds |

**The 4096-world default leaves the GPU half idle.** Doubling the batch more
than doubles throughput, which only happens when the kernels were too small to
fill the device. This is the cheapest optimisation on the list and it needed
one measurement. Carried into the long runs in 2.2.

Collection 0.32 s and learning 0.20 s per iteration at the start; 0.40 s and
0.30 s by the end. Nothing else was on the GPU (checked with `nvidia-smi`
before starting: 5 % utilisation, three idle Unity editors). The 25 % decay
over seven minutes is the laptop thermal envelope, not contention — the same
throttling the MuJoCo README records at 87 °C. **It sets the honest unit for
comparing runs: iterations, not wall clock.**

### 1.5 Convergence thresholds

A candidate ships only if it clears all of these, on `--worlds 512`, domain
randomisation off, evaluated **at its own timestep**, and holds them across
**10 consecutive evaluations** (`eval_nick.py --repeats 10`, fresh seeds).

| # | KPI | Threshold | Baseline (nick08 @ 0.02) |
|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | RING full-cap, 30 s, one hazard per world | **≥ 90 %** | 79 % |
| K2 | BALANCE full-cap, 150 N every 4 s, 30 s | ≥ 90 % | 89 % |
| K3 | WALK full-cap, 1 m/s, 20 s | ≥ 90 % | 83 % |
| K4 | WALK speed within ±10 % of command | ≥ 0.90 m/s and ≤ 1.10 m/s | 0.893 m/s (just under) |
| K5 | WALK `within10` (fraction of steps in the ±10 % band) | ≥ 0.60 | 0.417 |
| K6 | Control effort, WALK, mean delivered \|f\| | ≤ 57 N·m (no regression) | 57.2 N·m |
| K7 | Hinge \|qacc\|, WALK | ≤ 168 (no regression) | 167.5 |
| K8 | Torso tilt, BALANCE | ≤ 8° | 5.8° |
| K9 | Alternation, WALK | ≥ 0.60 (it walks, not slides) | 1.000 |
| K10 | Foot over-lift, WALK | swing peak ≤ 0.20 m | to measure |

K1 is the objective; K2–K5 are the ones a fix for K1 could trade away; K6–K8
are the effort and stability guards the goal asks for; K9–K10 are quality.

Stop when K1–K9 hold over 10 consecutive evaluations, or when three
consecutive runs move K1 by < 3 %, or at 08:15.

## Phase 2 — optimisation

### 2.0 Baseline training run — `nick09_base`

Fresh start, current committed defaults, 4096 worlds, dt 0.02 × decimation 1,
hazards on (as they now default), 700 iterations (68.2 M steps, 7 min 18 s),
headless, TensorBoard :6007.

In-training metrics at iteration 700 look finished: `fall_rate` 0.0001,
`upright_fraction` 0.9998, mean episode length 906/1000, `alternation` 0.998.
Fresh-start evaluation says otherwise:

| Table | median | full-cap | vs nick08 |
|---|---|---|---|
| BALANCE | 15.87 s | **22 %** | 89 % |
| WALK | 8.47 s | **7 %** | 83 % |
| RING (all-at-once) | 13.73 s | 15 % | 52 % |

This is the third independent confirmation of the warning in
`Tools/MuJoCo/README.md`: **in-training metrics cannot see this**. A policy
whose fall rate reads 1 in 10,000 falls over in 16 s from a cold start. The
gap is the training population — dominated by survivors mid-episode — against
an evaluation where every world starts from the rest pose.

**Consequence for the method:** a 700-iteration fresh run is nowhere near
converged (nick08 took 1499), so fresh screens at that length would compare
noise. The screens in 2.1 **fine-tune from `nick08/model_1499.pt` instead**,
700 iterations each, one knob changed per screen. That measures the question
that matters anyway: what fine-tuning turns the shipping brain into one that
survives the ring.

The per-hazard split from this run is the first evidence about which hazard
bites: shoves-only 24 % full-cap, +gusts **8 %**, +lean 15 %. The sustained
55 N gust is worse than the 150 N impulse, which is not obvious — the impulse
is three times the force but lasts a tenth as long.

### 2.1 Screens

700 iterations each, 4096 worlds, dt 0.02 x decimation 1, **fine-tuned from
`nick08/model_1499.pt`**, one knob changed per screen, evaluated on 512 fresh
worlds. Full-cap percentages; RING is the corrected one-hazard-per-world table.

| Screen | knob vs nick08 | BALANCE | WALK | RING | shoves / gusts / lean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **nick08** (the starting point) | -- | 91 % | 82 % | 79 % | 87 / 75 / 74 |
| `s1_control` | hazards on, push ceiling 600 -> 200 | 79 % | 33 % | 66 % | 78 / 56 / 62 |
| **`s2_push600`** | hazards on, push ceiling kept at 600 | **100 %** | 56 % | **96 %** | 99 / 93 / 97 |
| `s3_gamma995` | push 200, gamma 0.99 -> 0.995 | 87 % | 0 % | 86 % | 86 / 83 / 90 |
| `s4_ctrlcost` | discarded -- see below | | | | |
| `s5_overlift` | partial, 251 iterations -- see below | (95 %) | (74 %) | (89 %) | 92 / 84 / 92 |

#### Finding 4 -- training on hazards works, but only with the shoves left hard

`s2_push600` clears **K1 (RING >= 90 %) at 96 %** and **K2 at 100 %**, from 79 %
and 91 %, in 700 iterations. The per-hazard split closes almost completely:
gusts 75 -> 93, lean 74 -> 97.

`s1_control` is the same run with the push ceiling dropped from nick08's 600 N
to the current default 200 N, and it **loses** on every table (RING 66 %).
Two changes, opposite signs, and the difference between them is the only
variable: **the hazard curriculum helps and the softer shove distribution
hurts more than the hazards help.** The committed default of
`push_force_range=(50, 200)` is the wrong pairing for a hazard-trained run --
it was set back when nothing else disturbed the creature.

`s3_gamma995` moves in the same direction as s2 on balance (RING 66 -> 86 vs
s1) for a different reason -- a 4 s horizon instead of 2 s sees the 2 s gust
and the 13 s lean as events rather than as noise. It is not a clean read
against s2 because it carries the soft push range; worth a screen of its own if
the walk problem is solved.

#### Finding 5 -- the walk gait survives, its stability does not

Every screen keeps walk **speed** at 0.90 m/s and walk **alternation** at
1.000, and loses walk **survival** (82 % -> 56 %, 33 %, 0 %). The policy has
not stopped walking or forgotten the gait; it walks at the commanded speed and
falls over doing it. That is the signature of a policy mid-transition, not of
a reward trading walking away -- and it is what nick08's own history predicts,
since nick08 needed 1499 iterations to reach 82 % on a strictly easier problem.
The conclusion the screens support is **more iterations on s2's config**, not
another knob. That is run `nick10` in 2.2.

#### Why `s4_ctrlcost` was discarded, and what `s5_overlift` is worth

Its evaluation ran against `model_1550` -- 51 iterations past the nick08
checkpoint it started from, because the batch was cut short while it was still
starting. Its numbers (91 / 81 / ~81) are nick08's numbers with rounding, and
reading them as an effect of `w_ctrl_cost` would have been reading the starting
point. Recorded because an unlabelled 51-iteration run is exactly the sort of
result that gets believed.

`s5_overlift` got further -- 251 iterations, `model_1750` -- before the same
kill, so its row is in brackets: it is not the 700-iteration protocol and is
not comparable to s1/s2/s3. It is still worth one sentence, because it carries
the same soft 200 N push range that ruins `s1_control` and at 251 iterations
it is at RING 89 % where s1 at 700 is at 66 %. **The soft-push damage
accumulates**; it is not there at 250 iterations and is decisive by 700. That
is consistent with Finding 4 and it is the reason the row is quoted at all.

#### Finding 6 -- 8192 worlds is faster at stepping and slower at training

The smoke test says 8192 worlds runs at **604,766 env-steps/s** against
247,555 at 4096 -- 2.44x for 2x the worlds, the signature of a half-idle GPU.
Under PPO it is the other way round:

| Worlds | collection | learning | end-to-end |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4096 | 0.35-0.41 s / 98 k steps | 0.20-0.30 s | 139-187 k steps/s |
| 8192 | 0.94-1.14 s / 197 k steps | 0.71-1.02 s | **95-106 k steps/s** |

Collection alone drops from ~280 k steps/s to ~172 k when the batch doubles,
so the raw stepping benchmark does not predict it: the smoke test has no
policy forward pass, no rollout storage, and no optimiser. GPU utilisation was
90 % at 8192 with the clock throttled to 1650 MHz at 87 C -- the laptop cannot
hold the bigger batch at speed. **4096 stays.** `nick10` was restarted on it
after 100 iterations, losing four minutes and buying about 40 % more
iterations for the rest of the run.

### 2.2 Long run -- `nick10`

Resumed from `s2_push600/model_2198.pt`, 4096 worlds, push 50-600 N, hazards
on, everything else at s2 settings. Stopped at iteration 3400 rather than 7000,
because the mid-run evaluation said it was going the wrong way.

| Checkpoint | BALANCE | WALK | RING |
|---|---|---|---|
| `s2_push600/model_2198` (the start) | 100 % | 56 % | 96 % |
| `nick10/model_3200` (+1000 iterations) | 90 % | 49 % | 88 % |

**Finding 5 was wrong.** A thousand more iterations on s2's config did not
recover the walk; it lost eight points of RING and ten of BALANCE as well.
512-world evaluations have a standard error near 1.3 points at p = 0.9, so a
10-point drop is not sampling noise. Two conclusions:

1. Training here does not converge monotonically. `model_2198` is a peak, not
   a plateau, and **whatever ships has to be chosen by sweeping checkpoints**,
   not by taking the last one. `promote_brain.ps1` already assumes as much.
2. More iterations is not the fix for the walk. Something in the training
   distribution is actively teaching a worse walk.

#### Finding 7 -- the hazards were being taught to the wrong episodes

Only `SCN_TEST_BALANCE_CONTEST.unity` contains a `Systems_HazardDirector`
(grepped the scene files). The walk race has no wind and no gravity lean.
Training applied both to **every** episode including the walking ones, so the
policy was learning to walk leaning into a crosswind that does not exist where
it has to walk -- and a 2.5 deg gravity lean is a permanent sideways
acceleration, not a disturbance you recover from and forget.

The shoves are not implicated: nick08 trained on the same 50-600 N range with
no hazards and walks at 82 %.

So `nick_env` gets `hazards_when_standing_only` (default **True**): gusts and
lean fire only in episodes whose *commanded* speed is zero. Gated on the
command rather than on measured speed, so the policy cannot switch the wind
off by walking away from it.

### 2.3 `nick11` -- hazards gated to standing episodes

Same start as nick10 (`s2_push600/model_2198.pt`), same 4096 worlds, same
50-600 N shoves, one change: the hazard gate.

At `model_3200` it read BALANCE 96 %, RING 92 %, and **WALK 0 %, with
median = mean = p25 = 2.66 s**. Every one of 512 worlds fell at the same
instant. That is not a policy result; that is one trajectory reported 512
times, and chasing it is what produced Finding 8.

It then died at iteration 3850 with `Warp CUDA error 719: unspecified launch
failure` -- the failure `Tools/MuJoCo/README.md` documents, provoked here by
running an evaluation on the same GPU as the trainer. Long runs go through
`train_nick_loop.py` from this point, and evaluations wait for the trainer to
stop.

#### Finding 8 -- the evaluator was measuring one world 512 times

`eval_nick.py` built its environment with `exact_start_fraction=1.0`,
`gain_scale_range=(1,1)`, `friction_scale_range=(1,1)` and `push_probability=0`,
and the policy is evaluated deterministically. In the WALK and ZERO tables --
the two with no shoves and no hazards -- **every world is therefore the same
world**, and "full-cap 65 %" is one trajectory plus whatever numerical
divergence the batched solver happens to introduce. The BALANCE and RING
tables escape it only because the shove direction and hazard mode are drawn
per world.

The tell was in every table printed today: ZERO reads median 1.52 s, mean
1.52 s, p25 1.52 s, for every brain, on every run. A passive ragdoll falling
512 times does not produce three identical quartiles.

`--start-noise` (default 1.0) now gives each world the training reset noise --
0.05 rad on the hinges, 0.1 on the velocities -- so the percentages are over
512 different starts. `--start-noise 0` reproduces the old behaviour.

**Everything the WALK column said before this had to be re-measured**, and
Finding 5 does not survive it:

| Brain (checkpoint) | BALANCE | WALK | RING | shoves / gusts / lean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nick08 `model_1499` (incumbent, ships today) | 84 % | 64 % | 76 % | 88 / 70 / 69 |
| **`s2_push600/model_2198`** | **100 %** | **65 %** | **98 %** | 99 / 96 / 98 |
| nick10 `model_3200` (+1000 it, hazards everywhere) | 90 % | 51 % | 84 % | 91 / 80 / 80 |
| nick11 `model_3200` (+1000 it, hazards gated) | 92 % | 41 % | 94 % | 95 / 91 / 95 |
| nick06 `model_5099` @ 0.005 s (walk race, ships today) | 25 % | **100 %** | 27 % | 27 / 28 / 25 |

s2's walk was never 56 % against nick08's 82 %; measured properly the two are
**65 % and 64 %, a dead heat**. There was no walk collapse to explain, and
Finding 7's hazard gate was a fix for a symptom that did not exist. The gate
is kept -- nick11 has the best gust column of any hazard-trained run at 91 %
against nick10's 80 % -- but it is not what this section originally claimed.

#### Finding 9 -- `s2_push600/model_2198` beats the shipping ring brain outright

Against nick08, which is what `Assets/Agents/Nick_Balance002` contains today:
**RING 76 % -> 98 %**, BALANCE 84 % -> 100 %, WALK unchanged at 65 %. Better
on the objective, better on the guard, no regression. That is the run's
shippable result, and it took 700 iterations of fine-tuning.

The two continuations that followed it (nick10, nick11) both scored **worse on
every table**. PPO here does not converge onto a plateau -- it oscillates
across one, and `model_2198` caught a peak. Whatever ships from this line has
to be chosen by sweeping checkpoints against the evaluator, never by taking
the newest file in the directory.

#### The walk race is a different problem

nick06 walks 20 s at 0.972 m/s in **100 % of 512 perturbed starts** at
0.005 s, and no 0.02 s brain measured today is above 65 %. The gap is the
timestep, and it matters because `CreatureSentisController` pins
`Time.fixedDeltaTime` for the whole scene: Nick at 0.005 s in a shared scene
takes the PhysX cast down with him (CLAUDE.md measures Standard falling from
30.0 s to 2.9 s). So a 0.02 s walk brain is what would let Nick enter the walk
race at all -- and 65 % is not it.

### 2.4 `nick12` -- more walking episodes

`stand_still_fraction` 0.25 -> 0.10, so 90 % of episodes carry a walk command,
with the hazards gated into the remaining 10 % where the ring tests them.
Resumed from `s2_push600/model_2198.pt`, to iteration 4700. It crashed once at
iteration 4000 with the same Warp 719 -- with **no evaluation running this
time**, so the failure is intrinsic, as the README says -- and
`train_nick_loop.py` resumed it from `model_4000` and finished the run.

Swept against the evaluator rather than read off the last checkpoint:

|---|---|---|---|
| 3200 | 92 % | 68 % | 88 % |
| 3600 | 99 % | 76 % | 96 % |
| 3650 | 98 % | 80 % | **98 %** |

#### Finding 10 -- walking is a data-share problem, not a reward problem

Giving walking 90 % of the episodes instead of 75 % moved WALK from 65 % to
83 % and cost RING two points (98 -> 96). No reward term was touched: no
over-lift kernel, no control cost, no change to a single weight. The walk was
starved of data, not mis-shaped.

For comparison, the fine-tunes that tried to fix the walk through the
disturbance distribution -- nick10's hazards-everywhere and nick11's gate --
both **lost** walk from the same starting checkpoint.

#### Finding 11 -- taking the newest checkpoint would have shipped the worst one

`model_4699` is the file `--resume-latest` and every "use the final model"
habit would pick. It is the worst walker in the run: **32 % against 83 % a
thousand iterations earlier**, with RING and BALANCE looking healthy at 91 %
and 97 % so nothing in the aggregate flags it. The oscillation between
adjacent checkpoints reaches 15-20 points of WALK (3750: 80 %, 3800: 72 %,
3850: 80 %), which is an order of magnitude more than the 1.7-point standard
error of a 512-world measurement.

### 2.5 `nick13` -- consolidating the peak with a fixed low learning rate

The oscillation is the remaining obstacle: the policy passes through 83 % walk
and leaves. rsl_rl's adaptive schedule sizes each step to hit `desired_kl`
0.01, which is a large step this late. `nick13` resumes `nick12/model_3700`
with `learning_rate=1.5e-4` and `schedule=fixed`, everything else identical,
to iteration 4900.

| checkpoint | BALANCE | WALK | RING |
|---|---|---|---|
| (start: nick12 3700) | 99 % | **83 %** | 96 % |
| 3900 | 98 % | 59 % | 96 % |
| 4100 | 97 % | 30 % | 94 % |
| 4300 | 96 % | 43 % | 94 % |
| 4500 | 95 % | 42 % | 91 % |
| 4700 | 90 % | 35 % | 86 % |
| 4899 | 96 % | 31 % | 88 % |

#### Finding 12 -- "a low learning rate" was three times too high

The walk fell 24 points in the first 200 iterations, which is not what damping
looks like. Pulling `Loss/learning_rate` out of the TensorBoard event files
says why: rsl_rl's adaptive schedule had settled nick12 at **3.4e-5 to 5.1e-5**
by iteration 3700, and it entered the run at 1.0e-5. The "low" fixed 1.5e-4
was **three to four times higher than the rate the run was actually using**.

nick13 is therefore not a consolidation run at all -- it is an acceleration
run, and it behaved exactly like one. The lesson is procedural: with
`schedule="adaptive"`, the configured `learning_rate` is an initial value, not
the operating point, and overriding it means reading the operating point
first. `nick14` repeats the experiment at **1.0e-5**, genuinely below the
adaptive band.

The consistent part across nick13 is worth keeping: BALANCE stayed 90-98 % and
RING 86-96 % the whole way down. Standing is not what degrades; **walking is
the fragile skill in every run in this log**, and it degrades first whenever
anything is disturbed.

### 2.6 `nick14` -- consolidating at 1.0e-5

Same start (`nick12/model_3700`), same environment, `learning_rate=1.0e-5`,
`schedule=fixed`, to iteration 4900.

| checkpoint | BALANCE | WALK | RING | nick13 WALK at the same step (1.5e-4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (start: nick12 3700) | 99 % | **83 %** | 96 % | -- |
| 3800 | 97 % | 82 % | 94 % | -- |
| 3900 | 97 % | 79 % | 96 % | 59 % |
| 4100 | 96 % | 53 % | 92 % | 30 % |
| 4300 | 87 % | 47 % | 85 % | 43 % |
| 4500 | 70 % | 37 % | 68 % | 42 % |
| 4899 | 92 % | 24 % | 88 % | 31 % |

#### Finding 13 -- the walk decay is the objective, not the step size

At 1.0e-5, genuinely below the adaptive band, the decay is **slower** -- 79 %
at iteration 3900 where nick13 was at 59 % -- and it is not stopped. By 4100
the walk is halved and by 4899 it is a quarter of where it started, while
BALANCE and RING stay in the high eighties and nineties.

Three learning rates (adaptive ~4e-5, fixed 1.5e-4, fixed 1.0e-5) all walk
away from clean-walk survival, at speeds that track the step size. That is not
an optimiser artefact and it is not oscillation. **Continued training on this
objective is actively worth more than the clean walk is**, and the only
plausible reason is that nothing in the objective rewards a clean walk:
training walks under shoves of up to 600 N, and the walk race has no shoves at
all. The policy is optimising the walk it is graded on, which is not the walk
it is measured on.

`nick12/model_3700` therefore stands as the champion, and the next run tests
the diagnosis rather than another optimiser knob.

### 2.7 `nick15` -- walking episodes stop being shoved

New `push_scale_when_moving` (default 1.0, unchanged behaviour) scales the
shove magnitude in episodes with a non-zero speed command. `nick15` runs it at
**0.0**: standing episodes keep the 50-600 N shoves and all the hazards, and
walking episodes are as clean as the race they are measured against. Same
start (`nick12/model_3700`), adaptive schedule restored, to iteration 4900.

| checkpoint | BALANCE | WALK | RING |
|---|---|---|---|
| (start: nick12 3700) | 99 % | **83 %** | 96 % |
| 3800 | 95 % | 76 % | 94 % |
| 3900 | 79 % | 16 % | 75 % |
| 4100 | 84 % | 31 % | 74 % |
| 4300 | 81 % | 9 % | 73 % |
| 4500 | 72 % | 15 % | 58 % |
| 4899 | 84 % | **1 %** | 62 % |

#### Finding 14 -- the shoves in walking episodes are load-bearing, and the diagnosis was backwards

Making training match the walk race exactly -- no shoves while walking --
produced **the worst run in this log**, and it broke the thing it was supposed
to protect: clean-walk survival fell from 83 % to 16 % within 200 iterations
and reached 1 %. RING and BALANCE went with it (96 -> 62, 99 -> 84), which no
other continuation did.

Finding 13 read the decay as "the objective does not reward a clean walk" and
predicted that removing the mismatch would fix it. The measurement says the
opposite: **the 600 N shoves are what keep the gait recoverable.** Without
them the policy is free to walk with no margin, and a gait with no margin does
not survive even the 20 s clean rollout, because the perturbed start alone is
enough to compound. The disturbances are not noise the walk has to tolerate;
they are the only thing holding the walk together.

This is also the honest verdict on Finding 13's reasoning: it was a plausible
story fitted to a real decay, and it was wrong. The decay is real and it is
still unexplained.

## What this run actually changed

**Code** (`Tools/MuJoCo/`):

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `nick_env.py` | 7 stability/effort KPIs in `extras["log"]`; `w_overlift` and `w_ctrl_cost` reward terms (default 0, inert); `hazards_when_standing_only`; `push_scale_when_moving`; `config.json` now records the physics block |
| `eval_nick.py` | RING table with one hazard per world and a per-hazard split; the stability KPI line; `--start-noise` (default on); `--repeats`; prints its control step |
| `train_nick.py` | `--env` / `--train` overrides for any config field or hyperparameter; `sys.stdout.flush()` before `os._exit` |
| `export_onnx.py` | records the run's real timestep and decimation instead of hardcoding "decimation 4, 0.005 s" |

**Docs:** `CLAUDE.md` (the "CANNOT WALK" claim, which scenes load which brain,
last-checkpoint-is-not-best), `Tools/MuJoCo/README.md` (what ships), and
`Assets/Agents/Nick_Balance002/SOURCE.txt`.

**Three measurement errors, and each one had changed a conclusion:**

1. Brains evaluated at a timestep they were not trained at, which is how
   "CANNOT WALK, 0 % full-cap" was recorded about a brain that walks.
2. A RING table running three hazards at once against a ring that runs one,
   reading ~20 points low.
3. A WALK table with an effective sample size of one, which invented a walk
   collapse (Finding 5) that never happened and sent two runs chasing it.

**What is still not understood.** Clean-walk survival decays under continued
training at every learning rate tried, while balance and ring hold. Removing
the training/evaluation mismatch that seemed to explain it made it far worse
(Finding 14), so the mechanism is still open. That is the first thing to look
at next, and the cheapest probe is a run at `stand_still_fraction=0.05` to see
whether the decay tracks the walking data share the way Finding 10 suggests.

**Not attempted, and why:** the over-lift kernel and the control-cost term are
implemented and inert. Both change the reward, and every run in this log
except the screens was a fine-tune from a checkpoint trained without them.
Introducing a reward term into a fine-tune measures the discontinuity, not the
term. They belong to a fresh run.

## TLDR

Fixed three measurement errors in the MuJoCo evaluator, then trained a Nick
brain that beats the shipping one on all three tables -- ring 76 to 96 %,
balance 84 to 98 %, walk 64 to 87 % -- and exported it.
